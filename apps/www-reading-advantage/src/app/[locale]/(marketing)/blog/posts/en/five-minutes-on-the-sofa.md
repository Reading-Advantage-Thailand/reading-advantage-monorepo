After dinner, a mother sits at one end of the sofa. Her daughter settles beside her, still humming a tune from the day.

The mother opens the week's Storytime Advantage parent page on their phone. She taps “Hello, I am Pip.” The song starts, and her daughter sings before the first line ends.

Then they try the week's sound.

“Nnn,” says her mother.

“Nnn,” her daughter repeats.

The mother touches her nose. “Nnnose.”

They find one nut in the kitchen. “Nnnut.”

Back on the sofa, her daughter says, “Hello, I am ___,” and puts her own name in the space.

Five minutes. One song, one sound, and one short conversation. The phone goes back on the table.

This is the family evening we are building toward. Storytime Advantage launches on 30 November 2026. A parent needs one phone and a few quiet minutes after dinner.

## The week fits on one phone

The short parent page is in Thai. It shows the sound of the week, three words, one sentence, and a 5-minute home activity. A parent signs in on the first visit. The phone then stays signed in.

In week 1, the sound is n /n/: “nnn,” not the letter name “en.” The words are net, nut, and nose. The sentence is “Hello, I am ___.”

The parent does not need to plan an English lesson. The page gives her the next step, so she can stay with her child.

## Two books wait beside the song

Each week also has two books on the phone. The read-along book reads page by page. A child can choose “Read to me” or “I read,” tap a word to hear it, or use slow mode.

The decodable book gives her another way to join. She can tap its words and hear them. A word she does not know no longer means she must wait for an adult to say it.

At school, the class teacher can open the same weekly song and books on a classroom screen. The teacher signs in on the first visit, and that screen stays signed in too.

School and home share one clear weekly focus: one sound, one song with a video, and two books.

## Five minutes that belong to them

A parent may worry that English at home means another long task. This activity asks for five minutes. The parent reads the Thai steps, plays the sound, and joins one small exchange.

The child gets to sing, listen, point, speak, and laugh with someone she loves. The parent gets a clear way to take part without becoming an English teacher.

That is the vision of Storytime Advantage: a simple weekly routine that connects the classroom screen and the family phone.

One phone. One song. Five minutes on the sofa.
