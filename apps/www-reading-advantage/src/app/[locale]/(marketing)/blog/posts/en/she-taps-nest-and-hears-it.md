Early evening in a Thai home. A five-year-old sits at the kitchen table with the family phone. Behind her, a pan clicks on the stove. On the screen, a cartoon girl in an orange shirt points to a nest in a tree. Blue eggs sit in it. Underneath, one line: "A nest."

She stops at "nest." It is the one word on the line she does not know.

Her finger comes down on the word. The phone says it. She says it back — "Nnnest." Then she turns the page by herself.

From the stove, her parent smiles. Nobody had to stop cooking.

## One word, and she keeps going

A five-year-old who meets a word she does not know has two choices: wait for an adult, or stop reading. Storytime Advantage gives her a third. Tap the word. Hear it. Say it back. Turn the page.

The tap takes one finger. It needs no pause in the cooking and no break in the reading. That is the decodable book, and it arrives on the phone every week.

Each week the class learns one sound. Week 1 of Level 2 is n /n/ — said "nnn," not the letter name "en." The decodable page shows the sound with a button that plays it, a picture, and one line. The child can read that line with what she already knows.

The decodable book uses only the sounds the class has learned, so she can work out the line. The tap is there for the moment she gets stuck. She is not stuck and she is not waiting. She reads on.

## The week follows her home

Storytime Advantage launches on 30 November 2026. It is an app: the week's song, the week's books, and a parent page on a phone. In week 1, the song is "Hello, I am Pip." It is for children from Anuban 2 to P2.

You sign in on the first visit. The phone then stays signed in, so your next visit is just opening the page.

At school, the teacher opens the week on the classroom screen. At home, you open the same week on the family phone. The vision is a simple weekly routine that connects the classroom screen and the family phone. Both places hold one week: one sound, one song, and the books she can tap.

## Nobody stops cooking

You may worry that English at home means one more screen to manage. This one asks you for nothing but the first sign-in. Your child reads a short book made of sounds she knows, taps the word she does not, and keeps going.

Nobody leans over her shoulder. The kitchen goes on around her.

We are building for the evening when every adult in the house is busy and a child keeps going anyway. Each small win is hers.
