# Meet Cartoon: Bringing Vocabulary Practice to a Playful Zombie Graveyard

Great product ideas can come from anywhere in a company, especially from the people who spend every day helping users.

**Cartoon**, a 20-year-old member of the Reading Advantage helpdesk team, entered our LLM-First Game Competition with a concept full of personality: a vocabulary challenge set inside a moonlit graveyard, populated by wide-eyed zombies, moving distractions, bombs, and a player-controlled cannon.

The prototype turns a familiar English-learning task into a scene that feels playful, immediate, and easy to understand.

## From Helping Users to Designing for Learners

Helpdesk work builds a valuable instinct: notice what users see, understand where they hesitate, and communicate clearly. Cartoon brought that same user-centered perspective into the game.

The learning loop is visible at a glance. A Thai meaning appears at the top of the screen. English words are attached to zombie characters across the graveyard. The player's job is to identify the matching term and select the correct target before the timer runs out.

During gameplay, a prompt such as **ความแข็งแกร่ง** appears while words including _journey_, _success_, _strength_, and _knowledge_ are spread across the field. The correct answer is part of the environment, so reading and recognition happen inside the action rather than on a separate test screen.

## Friendly Monsters, Clear Feedback

The graveyard may be dark, but the visual tone is welcoming. Cartoon's zombies have expressive eyes, rounded shapes, and different colors that make the field feel more playful than frightening.

The prototype adds variety through several enemy behaviors:

- normal zombies that remain steady targets;
- shy zombies that fade in and out;
- fast zombies that move more aggressively;
- shield zombies that require an extra hit;
- bombs that create a time penalty;
- bats that cross the screen as visual distractions.

Correct choices trigger points, particles, and combo progress. The timer and score remain visible, giving the player immediate feedback about both accuracy and momentum.

## A Game Designed Around Repetition

Vocabulary mastery depends on repeated retrieval, but repetition does not need to feel repetitive. Cartoon's design creates a reason to scan the field again and again. Each new Thai prompt changes the target, while moving characters and obstacles keep the visual search fresh.

The combo system adds another layer of motivation. Consecutive correct answers build toward stronger labels such as **GOOD**, **GREAT**, and **INCREDIBLE**. That transforms a sequence of vocabulary questions into a personal challenge: how long can the player maintain focus and accuracy?

It is a strong example of game-based learning because the reward is tied directly to the learning behavior.

## Creativity from Inside Reading Advantage

Cartoon's entry is also a reminder that innovation is not limited to a product or engineering department. A helpdesk team member who understands the daily experience of learners, teachers, and customers can bring a valuable perspective to educational design.

During the competition, Cartoon combined that perspective with modern development tools to build a distinctive concept in only eight hours. The result includes responsive layouts, animated characters, scoring, varied enemy types, and a complete visual theme centered on vocabulary recognition.

Most importantly, the prototype has warmth. It takes a potentially intimidating scene and fills it with friendly characters, readable choices, and a clear invitation to try again.

At Reading Advantage, we want every team member to feel that they can contribute ideas to the future of learning. Cartoon's zombie graveyard shows why: creativity grows when people closest to users are given space to design, experiment, and build.
