At pick-up, a parent waits at the classroom door. Her daughter is still inside, pointing at a wall covered with paper cards.

“What comes next for my child?” the parent asks.

The teacher walks to the wall. Each card holds a skill. Drawn lines and strings connect each skill to the skills it needs.

The girl points to one card near the center. Her teacher smiles.

“She is ready for this one. These are the skills that lead to it.”

The parent looks at the path. Her daughter’s next lesson has a place. The steps that brought her here have a place too.

## A map made of skills

This is the classroom we are building with Mastery Advantage, the engine under every Advantage book and its digital twin.

Knowledge Space Theory maps a subject as a graph of skills and their prerequisites. The engine uses that map to find the skills a student is ready to learn next. FSRS then times each review just before the student would forget.

One part decides **what to learn next**. The other decides **when to review**.

For a teacher, that changes the shape of the pick-up conversation. The answer can begin with one child and one reachable skill. The teacher can show the parent where that skill sits and which earlier skills support it.

The map also gives review a clear purpose. A child returns to an earlier skill at the right moment, then continues along her path.

Mastery Advantage puts this work in four words: **“Always know what’s next.”**

## Every child can see a way forward

Our goal is a child who never meets a lesson she has no way to reach.

She opens her book and finds the lesson her teacher has prepared. She continues the story waiting on her screen. As the engine enters Primary Advantage, her path can grow from the skills she holds toward the skills she is ready to meet.

Her teacher gains a clearer answer at the classroom door. Her parent gains a clearer picture of the road ahead. She gains a next step she can see.

A lesson ends. The path continues.
