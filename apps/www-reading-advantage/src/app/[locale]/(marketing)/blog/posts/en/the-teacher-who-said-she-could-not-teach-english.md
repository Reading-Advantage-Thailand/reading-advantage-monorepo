"I teach P4. I do not teach English. I cannot."

Khru Nok said it plainly, in the director's office, with the timetable on the wall behind her. The timetable had an empty box where English belonged. Nobody was free to fill it.

On Sunday evening, she opens the class book on her laptop. She switches on rehearsal mode and reads the first lesson aloud to her living room, one step at a time. The projector view shows her exactly what the children will see. The 13-step guide comes in English and Thai, so she always knows what each step is for and what to say next.

On Monday morning, she stands at the front of the room with Primary Advantage Origins 3.1 open in her hand. Every child has the same page open on the desk. She reads the first line of Before You Read. The children answer. She turns to the next step, and the next.

Somewhere around the third step, she notices something she did not expect. She is no longer worrying about her English. She is watching faces.

## Thirteen steps, printed and fixed

Every lesson in the book follows the same thirteen steps, in the same order. It begins with Before You Read and Key Vocabulary. It moves through the article, the notes, the questions, and the writing. It closes with Lesson Reflection. Every book holds fourteen lessons.

Nothing is left to invent at 8:40 on a Monday. The plan is printed, and the plan does not change.

## Why a fixed plan frees a teacher

A scripted lesson gives a teacher something to stand on. Her skill goes where it matters most: to the child who hesitates, to the child who is ready for more, to the quiet corner that needs a smile.

That is the idea behind Blended Learning at Reading Advantage: printed workbooks plus the app. Each printed step matches a step in the app, so what a child writes with a pencil on Monday is what she opens on a screen at home. The workbook comes first. The thinking happens on paper, and the teacher can see it happening.

A good English classroom does not depend on one person's accent. It depends on a clear sequence, a teacher who knows her children, and a book that holds the two together.
