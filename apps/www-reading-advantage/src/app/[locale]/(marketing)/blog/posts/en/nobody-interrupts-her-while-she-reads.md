The children are reading, and the room is quiet.

The teacher is not walking the rows with a red pen. Nobody asks what "park" means. Nobody quizzes a child halfway down the page. In the third row, a girl runs a finger under the line and keeps going.

Her workbook page says **Read the Article**. For the next ten minutes, the article is hers.

## One step where the child leads

Primary Advantage is foundation-building extensive reading for grades 3 to 6 English. Each printed workbook holds 14 lessons. Every lesson follows the same 13 printed steps, and Read the Article is step 3. It comes after Before You Read and Key Vocabulary, so she opens the page already holding the first words.

The quiet is on purpose. Uninterrupted Reading is the first pillar of the Big 4 classroom-fidelity protocol, alongside Student Agency, Data-Driven Consensus, and Workbook-First Protocol. For a Thai teacher, that gives these ten minutes one clear job: guard the quiet. There is no English question to answer and no correction to make.

The page also carries a small legend of reading marks: new word, confusing, interesting, save sentence. So when she meets a word she does not know, she marks it and reads on. The mark waits for step 4, Collect Vocabulary, where it becomes work.

## The door in the page

At the bottom of that page sits a QR code. Beside it: "SCAN TO READ IN THE APP. Access the interactive article with audio, vocabulary pop-ups, and more."


She scans it, and the same article opens on screen. There she can read it, listen to it, and answer questions. That means the sound of the English arrives when she is ready for it, and no adult has to stand beside her.

You may worry that this is one more screen. The order answers that. The workbook comes first, and the app opens from the page. The book leads, and the screen follows.

## A library that goes home

Now picture the evening. The workbook is in her school bag. The QR code is on a page she can open at the kitchen table. This library has no closing bell, and it fits in a school bag.

We are building Primary Advantage toward one picture: a Thai child who keeps reading English after the lesson ends, because she likes to. Research shows extensive reading outperforms traditional grammar instruction (Aka, 2019). That is why reading gets the quiet and the time, and why the page is built to lead a child out of the classroom and into a habit.

The same idea is growing. In the 2027 school year, Thai primary schools add Science Advantage, Math Advantage, and Zhongwen Advantage: more books to hold, more subjects to read. A new Advantage app aims to open every month, and Storytime Advantage comes first, in November.
